From evidence to a decision

Every step is stored, so any decision can be traced back and compared with the one it replaced

  1. You loadAssets and their vulnerabilitiesFrom your inventory and scanner (CSV). Includes whether each asset faces the internet and how critical it is.
  2. It collectsEvidence of exploitationThe CISA KEV catalog, EPSS scores, reviewed advisories, and attacks your own sensors saw. Each item keeps its source and version.
  3. It judgesThe threat, one axis at a timeConfirmed or only reported? Attacked here or elsewhere? Independent sources or one rumour reposted ten times? Nothing is merged into a score.
  4. It decidesAn action for each assetRespond to an incident, patch, investigate, or monitor, with an urgency, the rule that applied, and what is still unconfirmed.
  5. It explainsWhat changed and whyWhen evidence changes, the affected decisions are recomputed and shown before and after. Decisions that are not affected stay as they are.

Where to find answers

  • Threat Changes
    What changed recently and which decisions it moved.
  • Affected Assets
    The current action and urgency for every asset, with reasons.
  • Investigation Queue
    The few checks that would settle the most undecided cases.
  • Threats
    The evidence behind each vulnerability, including retracted or disputed claims.
  • Sources & Health
    Is the data fresh? Did a download fail? Stale data is flagged, never silently dropped.

Rules it never breaks

  • Unknown is not safe.
    Missing applicability, exposure or EPSS leads to "investigate", never to "monitor".
  • A scan is not an attack, and an alert is not a breach.
    Only a reviewed confirmation counts as a compromise.
  • Reposts are not new evidence.
    Articles citing the same source count as one.
  • History is never rewritten.
    Corrections and reviews are added; old versions remain.
  • Nothing leaves this machine.
    The only outbound requests are KEV/EPSS downloads that you start.
